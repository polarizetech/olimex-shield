# Disciplines

The research disciplines this organisation works in. The researcher, analyst and science-writer agents
read this file. It is generated by scientific-research-scaffold from the `polarizetech` profile, and
`scaffold update` keeps it current.

Claims marked [BK] are background knowledge, not verified in a session. Verify one before relying on it.

## Neuroscience (computational and systems)

Models of neurons, circuits and memory, and recordings of brain activity (EEG, MEG, LFP, spikes).

- **Established versus not.** Membrane biophysics (Hodgkin–Huxley-type conductance models, cable theory)
  is established. Most claims about how a circuit represents or stores information are theory, however
  often they are repeated. Say which one a claim is.
- **Simulators first.** Brian2, NEST, NEURON, GeNN and BrainPy each cover a model class; write glue, not
  engines (see the language-routing reference).
- **Data standards.** BIDS (and EEG-BIDS and MEG-BIDS) for recordings, NWB for cellular physiology, and
  NeuroML or PyNN for shareable models. [BK]
- **Reporting.** For M/EEG, the COBIDAS MEEG recommendations (Pernet et al., 2020) are the checklist. State
  the reference, filters, artefact handling and every exclusion. [BK]
- **Cautions.** Circular analysis (selecting and testing on the same data), uncorrected multiple
  comparisons across channels, time and frequency, and reading an oscillation into a filtered broadband
  signal.

## Cardiology (computational and electrophysiological)

ECG and heart-rate signals, cardiac cell and tissue models, and interactions between the heart and the brain.

- **Established versus not.** ECG waveform physiology and standard intervals are established. Most
  heart–brain (interoception) claims are active research, and "heartbeat-evoked" effects are sensitive to
  analysis choices.
- **Standards.** The AHA/ACCF/HRS recommendations for standardising and interpreting the ECG (Kligfield et
  al., 2007), and for heart-rate variability the Task Force of the ESC and NASPE (1996). Name the HRV
  measure, the window length and the artefact correction. [BK]
- **Data and models.** PhysioNet for open recordings (Goldberger et al., 2000), CellML for cell models, and
  openCARP for tissue simulation. [BK]
- **Cautions.** R-peak detection errors propagate into every HRV measure, so check them by eye on a sample.
  Respiration and posture confound HRV. Nothing built here is a diagnostic device; never word an output as
  a diagnosis.

## Auditory neuroscience and clinical electrophysiology

The auditory periphery and pathway, auditory evoked potentials (ABR, ASSR, cortical AEPs), tinnitus, and
recording from people with EEG, ECG and EMG.

- **Established versus not.** Cochlear tonotopy and the basic ABR wave sequence are established.
  Mechanisms of tinnitus and most proposed "signal delivery" routes are theory. Say which.
- **Models.** Auditory-periphery models such as Zilany, Bruce and Carney (2014), and gammatone
  filterbanks. Brian Hears for filterbanks inside Brian. [BK]
- **Stimuli.** Calibrate and report stimulus level with its reference (dB SPL, peSPL or dB nHL), and give
  rate, polarity and transducer. An uncalibrated level is not a result. [BK]
- **Recording.** Electrode positions in the 10–20 system (or 10–10), the reference, impedance, filters
  and sample rate, all written down. EEG-BIDS for storage. [BK]
- **Safety.** Anything connected to a person follows medical electrical safety practice (IEC 60601-1 for
  isolation and leakage current). Hobby hardware is not a medical device, and the tool must say so. Sound
  exposure must stay within safe limits. These never relax, at any stage. [BK]

## Bioelectricity (developmental and non-neural)

Resting membrane potential (Vmem), ion channels and gap junctions in non-neural cells, and bioelectric
signals in development, regeneration and pattern formation.

- **Established versus not.** That non-neural cells hold and change membrane potentials, and that ion
  channels and gap junctions shape them, is established. That Vmem patterns act as *instructive* signals
  for anatomy is a well-supported research programme, largely from a small number of labs, not settled
  science. Treat it as tier 2 at most, and say so.
- **Methods.** Voltage-reporting dyes (such as DiBAC4(3)) and genetically encoded voltage indicators,
  with their artefacts; ion-channel misexpression and pharmacology as interventions. [BK]
- **Models.** Tissue-level bioelectric simulators such as BETSE (Pietak and Levin, 2016); every parameter
  tagged by its source. [BK]
- **Cautions.** Voltage-dye signals are relative and confounded by cell volume and dye loading. Correlating
  a Vmem pattern with an outcome is not causation, and an intervention needs a control for off-target
  channel effects.
